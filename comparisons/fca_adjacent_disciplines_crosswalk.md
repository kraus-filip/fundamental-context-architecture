# FCA And Adjacent Disciplines

Status: supporting comparison note for FCA `1.0`
Authority: explanatory comparison; the kernel remains in `blueprint.md`

## Purpose

This document defines how FCA relates to adjacent architectural and governance disciplines and supporting implementation capabilities. It focuses on the governed contextual foundation required by an AI-first organization and explains how that foundation connects to existing architecture work.

The objective is to connect these responsibilities without creating parallel architecture work. Existing work is reused through established authority and change mechanisms. FCA makes the required contextual relationships explicit. Responsibilities specific to an adjacent discipline or capability remain with their established owners.

## Relationship Crosswalk

| Adjacent discipline or capability | Established role | Relationship to FCA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enterprise architecture and architecture description | Enterprise architecture defines how an organization is structured, designed, governed, and changed in response to stakeholder concerns. Architecture-description methods provide the viewpoints used to communicate that architecture, while repositories preserve the resulting work. ([The Open Group: TOGAF and ArchiMate](https://help.opengroup.org/hc/en-us/articles/32115987894930-How-the-ArchiMate-Language-and-the-TOGAF-Standard-Complement-Each-Other)) | FCA defines the governed contextual foundation required by an AI-first organization. That foundation applies throughout how the organization is designed, governed, operated, and changed. Where enterprise architecture already exists, the contextual foundation is established through the same architecture authority and change mechanisms. Existing architecture work is reused, while FCA makes the required contextual relationships explicit. Shared concerns are decided and recorded once rather than maintained through parallel architecture practices. |
| Data governance, data management, and data products | Data governance establishes responsibility for data and the conditions under which it remains trustworthy and usable. Data-management and data-product practices put those responsibilities into operation. ([ISO/IEC 38505-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/56639.html)) | FCA treats governed data as part of the contextual foundation rather than as its whole. Data governance remains responsible for the data. FCA connects that data to the authoritative meaning and applicable rules for the work in which people or AI-enabled systems use it. It preserves the evidence needed to explain what followed from that use. |
| Semantic layers, ontologies, taxonomies, and knowledge graphs | Semantic disciplines make organizational meaning explicit and consistent across people and systems. They define concepts, classifications, and relationships in representations that support interpretation, exchange, navigation, and reasoning. ([W3C OWL](https://www.w3.org/OWL/) and [W3C SKOS](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/)) | FCA uses semantic capabilities to establish governed vocabulary and relationships across its contextual foundation. It connects that meaning to source authority and lifecycle, then governs how context is used, promoted, evidenced, and held accountable. The implementation selects the representation appropriate to its scope. Controlled vocabularies and taxonomies support simpler meaning structures. Ontologies and knowledge graphs support richer relationships, interoperability, and reasoning. |
| Authorization, policy, and enforcement | Authorization establishes whether an identified person or system can access a resource or perform an operation under policy. Enforcement applies that decision at the point where access or action occurs. ([NIST: authorization](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/authorization) and [policy-enforcement point](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/policy_enforcement_point)) | FCA distinguishes access to context from permission to use it for a particular purpose. Transformation, promotion, boundary crossing, and action each remain separately governed. FCA connects those decisions to source authority, lifecycle state, and human approval. The implementation carries them into operation through its policy evaluation and enforcement capabilities. |
| Workflow, process, and decision methods | Workflow, process, and decision methods define how work progresses and how decisions are reached. They coordinate execution and make variations or escalation paths explicit. ([OMG BPMN](https://www.omg.org/bpmn/) and [OMG DMN](https://www.omg.org/dmn/)) | FCA connects this work to the governed context it uses and produces. It establishes the authoritative source and governed meaning for the work. It also determines which uses and boundary crossings are permitted and what evidence makes the result reviewable. When work produces useful new context, FCA provides the promotion path that determines whether it remains local or becomes governed and reusable. |
| AI governance, AI risk, and model governance | AI governance establishes accountability and controls for AI systems throughout their lifecycle, according to the risks created by their design, deployment, and use. ([NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework)) | FCA connects those system-level decisions to the organizational context the AI capability uses or affects. The capability's approved role and required oversight constrain how it participates in governed work. FCA also governs that contextual foundation when the work is performed by people or non-AI systems. |
| Retrieval, RAG, GraphRAG, memory, and agent orchestration | Retrieval and RAG bring external information into model-supported work. GraphRAG structures that retrieval around connected information. Memory preserves selected state across interactions or tasks. Agent orchestration coordinates execution across agents and the human intervention their work requires. | These capabilities operate within the contextual foundation governed by FCA. They make context available and actionable, but they do not establish its authority or governance status. Retrieved, remembered, or generated material becomes governed and reusable only through the applicable promotion decision. |
| Provenance, lineage, observability, and audit | Provenance and lineage preserve where information came from and how it changed. Observability makes execution behavior inspectable. Audit evaluates the resulting trace against applicable requirements. ([W3C PROV](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)) | FCA defines what the evidence of governed work must make reviewable. The evidence connects the context used to its source basis. It shows why the use was permitted and what decision, action, or outcome followed. It also preserves the result's lifecycle and reuse status. When FCA evidence becomes an organizational record, its retention and disposition follow the applicable [records-management rules](https://committee.iso.org/sites/tc46sc11/home/projects/published/iso-15489-records-management.html). |

## Implementation Through Existing Capabilities

FCA is implemented in organizations where existing practices and systems already carry parts of the contextual foundation. Implementation maps those capabilities to FCA responsibilities and identifies where required relationships are missing or fragmented.

Existing capabilities are reused when they fulfil the required role. Gaps are addressed through organizational or technical change. FCA defines the responsibilities and review criteria used to assess the resulting foundation; product categories do not define its architecture.

Adjacent methods and standards are incorporated according to the responsibilities they fulfil in that implementation. FCA remains defined by its architectural responsibilities rather than by adoption of any named method or technology.
